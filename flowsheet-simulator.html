Flowsheet Simulator — how to use

The full, illustrated user guide walks through a first flowsheet step by step and covers every unit, property method, results tab and common problem. This panel is the short version.

This is a free, browser-based process flowsheet simulator for steady-state material and energy balances. It works like the drawing board of a commercial simulator: choose a property method, place unit operations, connect them with material streams, specify the feeds and the units, and run. The whole flowsheet — calculation order, recycle loops, design specs, sensitivity studies, optimisation and costing — is solved by the multicalci.com engine in one call.

0 · Set up (optional)

New flowsheet on the start page (or File → New flowsheet…) asks for the components, recommends a property method for them with a one-line reason, and sets the units. Every new feed then lists those components, so only the amounts, flow, T and P need typing. Reopen it from the flowsheet panel (click empty canvas) to change any of the three.

1 · Place equipment

Drag a unit from the palette on the left onto the canvas (or click it to drop it in the middle). Feed arrows and product arrows are created automatically on its ports. Red port circles are required inlets that still need a stream; blue ones are optional or outlets.

2 · Connect streams

  • Drag the tip of an outlet arrow onto an inlet port of the next unit — the two units are now connected.
  • Or drag from any free port: release on another port to connect, or on empty canvas to make a feed or product.
  • Select a stream and drag its end handle to re-route it. On a phone use the Connections dropdowns in the unit's properties.
  • Energy streams (dashed arrows): point at a unit to see its ◆ energy port — orange for heat, purple for shaft work — and drag from it onto another unit's ◆ to pass that energy on: a cooler's heat into a heater, a turbine's power into the compressor it drives.

Energy streams

An energy stream carries heat (Q) or shaft work (W) from one unit to another. The unit at the arrow head takes the energy the unit at the tail gives up as its own specification: a heater, mixer, flash drum or three-phase separator takes it as its duty, a compressor (on its design point, with a property method) as its shaft power — so its discharge temperature and efficiency follow. Drag between two ◆ ports in either direction: the arrow always points at the unit that can receive. Dropped on empty canvas, the arrow shows the unit's calculated duty or power; select it and set From to none to give the unit a duty you type instead. A link can deliver a fraction of the energy (a gearbox efficiency, heat losses). Turbines, pumps and exchangers cannot receive energy streams, heat goes only to heat and work only to work, and each unit has one energy port. A receiver upstream of its source in the same process is solved by repeated passes and converges only when less energy comes back than goes in — for feed–effluent heat recovery use a heat exchanger. Energy streams appear in the Summary tab, the Excel workbook (Units sheet) and the report; the “Steam-turbine-driven compressor with heat recovery” example shows both kinds.

3 · Specify feeds

Click a feed arrow (blue label). Pick a typical stream (air, natural gas, steam…) or add components from the searchable dropdown, choose the basis (mole %, mass %, kmol/h, kg/h), then enter total flow, temperature and pressure in any unit — °C/K/°F, bar(a), bar(g), kg/cm²(g), psig, kPa, kmol/h, Nm³/h, t/h.

4 · Specify units and run

Click a unit and fill its specifications. The status lamp at the bottom turns green when everything needed is entered; the Issues tab lists anything missing. Press Run (or F5).

Property method (Methods)

Click empty canvas to open the flowsheet panel and pick a property method for the whole flowsheet — Peng–Robinson or SRK for hydrocarbons, light gases and NH₃ / syngas at pressure; GERG-2008 for natural gas, CO₂ and hydrogen blends at reference accuracy; a reference equation for one pure fluid (NH₃, CO₂, N₂, H₂, CH₄, Ar, O₂); NRTL, UNIQUAC or Wilson (with ChemSep binary parameters built in) for polar liquids; UNIFAC or Dortmund UNIFAC when no binary data exist; or ideal. With a property method, mixers, heaters, flash drums, exchangers, pumps, compressors, turbines, valves and conversion reactors become rigorous: every outlet is flashed, so streams can leave two-phase, and enthalpies include heats of vaporisation and mixing. Review pure-component & binary data shows exactly which constants and binary pairs will be used; you can override kij and NRTL pairs. With Peng–Robinson or SRK, Free water (on by default) puts liquid water and steam on IAPWS-IF97 and the gases dissolved in water on the IAPWS Henry constants, so boilers, condensers, wet-gas coolers and process condensate come out right — a plain cubic overstates the latent heat of water by about 5 % and makes gases 10–100× too insoluble in it. Peng–Robinson and SRK also shift the molar volume of H₂, N₂, CH₄, CO, Ar, O₂ and He (volume translation, on by default), so syngas densities and compression work agree with the reference equations of state. NRTL and UNIQUAC carry water–hydrocarbon pairs fitted to IUPAC-NIST mutual solubilities (benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, xylenes, cyclohexane, methylcyclohexane, C5–C8 alkanes), so decanters and heteroazeotropic columns split into the right two liquids. Set Valid phases to vapour–liquid–liquid for free water or partially miscible solvents; a bubble point with two liquids present is then the heterogeneous one. Activity methods can add the heat of mixing to the liquid enthalpy (reliable with Dortmund UNIFAC, which was fitted to it) and switch off the Poynting correction; Liquid volume chooses how densities, volumetric flows and pump heads are found — ChemSep correlations (default), the Rackett mixture rule, COSTALD or the equation of state's liquid root; the first three include the effect of pressure (Tait) and of light gases dissolved in the liquid. With an activity-coefficient method, carboxylic acids (acetic, propionic …) dimerise in the vapour (chemical theory) — vapour fugacities, densities and the liquid enthalpy of the acids then agree with measured data. Any rigorous unit can use its own method. Classic keeps the engine's per-unit models (the flash drum then offers Raoult, Peng–Robinson or NRTL individually).

With the activity and electrolyte methods the liquid enthalpy comes from liquid heat capacities (water from IAPWS-IF97), anchored at the normal boiling point; a checkbox in the flowsheet panel switches back to hig − ΔHvap(T), which overstates the liquid heat capacity of methanol, ethanol and ammonia by 10–27 %.

Which method to apply: water / steam only → Steam / water (IAPWS-IF97); hydrocarbons and light gases → Peng–Robinson or SRK; NRTL, UNIQUAC or UNIFAC for polar liquids above about 5 bar → set Vapour phase to Peng–Robinson. The flowsheet panel suggests the method from your feeds (with a button that applies it), and after a run the log warns when a vapour above about 5 bar was treated as an ideal gas — that leaves out the real-gas enthalpy and changes duties and turbine / compressor power (a 62 bar steam turbine comes out about 30 % high with NRTL and an ideal-gas vapour).

The component list holds about 2,770 components for the property methods: 431 with ChemSep pure-component data (blue dot), 1,118 more from evaluated literature data (purple dot) — every one with measured critical constants and boiling point — and 1,198 with critical constants from Yaws’ compilation (pink dot), each with a measured boiling point and vapour-pressure equation; refrigerants, siloxanes and other fluids with a reference equation of state take their vapour pressure, liquid density, heat of vaporisation and heat capacity from it. Review pure-component & binary data lists the source of every value; what was not measured is estimated (Joback heat capacity, Lee–Kesler vapour pressure through the measured Tb, Tc, Pc), as for a component given by its structure. Carbamate and biuret are for the classic urea units only. Pseudo-components (flowsheet panel) add petroleum fractions from their normal boiling point and specific gravity — critical constants by Twu, Riazi–Daubert or Kesler–Lee — or cut a whole TBP assay into fractions and use it as a feed composition; they work with the equations of state, Chao–Seader, Grayson–Streed and ideal.

User components (flowsheet panel) add any other organic molecule from its structure: paste its SMILES (from PubChem or a drawing program). Joback groups estimate the boiling point, critical constants, heat capacity and heat of formation; Lee–Kesler the acentric factor and vapour pressure; UNIFAC and Dortmund-UNIFAC groups are assigned so activity methods work at once. Enter any measured value you have — each replaces its estimate. With NRTL, UNIQUAC or Wilson, a binary pair with no data is fitted to Dortmund UNIFAC automatically (flagged in the run log and shown as estimated in Review pure-component & binary data). Viscosity, thermal conductivity and surface tension are estimated where the databank has no correlation; the stream panel says which.

Urea synthesis method

For the urea HP loop pick Urea synthesis: the liquid is UNIQUAC over NH₃, CO₂, H₂O, urea, ammonium carbamate and bicarbonate with the carbamate and bicarbonate equilibria solved in every flash (Voskov et al. 2016), the vapour Peng–Robinson. Streams carry apparent NH₃, CO₂, H₂O and urea, so N/C and H/C read as in the plant, and liquid streams show the conversion, carbamate share and true species. The urea reactor brings carbamate ⇌ urea + H₂O to equilibrium (or a fraction of it, or a given conversion), adiabatic or at a set temperature, and reports the inert-free bubble pressure and residence time; the HP stripper is a counter-current equilibrium-stage stripper heated by steam, reporting the stripping efficiency. Heaters, flash drums and mixers on this method act as carbamate condensers and scrubbers. The “Urea HP synthesis” example shows the section.

Steam turbines, compressors and the IAPWS-IF97 steam method

For water and steam pick the Steam / water (IAPWS-IF97) property method: every state — compressed water, wet steam, superheated and supercritical steam — comes from the full IAPWS-IF97 industrial formulation (the one steam tables and turbine vendors use), with IAPWS viscosity and thermal conductivity; it takes pure water only. Drag a pump / compressor / turbine onto the canvas, connect the inlet and outlet arrows, give the outlet pressure and choose the known value: the efficiency (outlet temperature and power are calculated), the measured outlet temperature (efficiency and power are calculated) or the shaft power (outlet state and efficiency are calculated), on an isentropic or polytropic basis. Turbines report the exhaust steam quality, a wetness warning above 12 % and the steam rate in kg/kWh. The extraction steam turbine has an HP section to the extraction pressure, a controlled extraction (flow or fraction) and an LP section to the exhaust, each with its own efficiency; it uses the IAPWS-IF97 method by default. The “Extraction steam turbine + condenser” example shows both.

Pumps and compressors can run on a performance curve instead of a design point: enter head, efficiency (and NPSHr for pumps) against flow at the rated speed; the curve sets the discharge pressure, another speed follows the affinity laws, and the results show the operating point on the curve, the share of best-efficiency flow, NPSH available against required, and the compressor's surge margin (a surge flag appears on the drawing). The multistage compressor splits the duty into equal-ratio stages with intercoolers and knock-out drums and tabulates each stage.

CO₂ removal — amine and hot-carbonate packages

Two electrolyte (acid-gas) methods model chemical CO₂ absorption. Aqueous MEA solves the carbamate and bicarbonate equilibria of monoethanolamine (the published Akula et al. model used by IDAES): CO₂ dissolves by Henry's law on the free, unreacted CO₂, water and MEA follow Raoult's law on the true species. Hot potassium carbonate (Benfield-type solutions) uses the Pitzer electrolyte model with the USGS PHREEQC parameters for K⁺, CO₃²⁻, HCO₃⁻, CO₂, OH⁻ and the water activity. Enter the solvent as its apparent components — MEA or K₂CO₃, water and CO₂ (the CO₂ sets the lean loading or conversion); H₂, N₂, CH₄, CO, Ar and O₂ dissolve physically. Heats of absorption follow from the solubility model (about −84 kJ/mol CO₂ in MEA, −30 kJ/mol in hot carbonate). Liquid streams show the loading or conversion to KHCO₃, the equilibrium CO₂ pressure and, for carbonate, the water activity. H₂S and NH₃ are not modelled. The two CO₂-removal examples build an absorber + regenerator with each solvent.

A third method, Aqueous MDEA, uses the Extended UNIQUAC electrolyte model (UNIQUAC + Debye–Hückel) with the parameters of Sadegh's DTU thesis (2013): true species H₂O, CO₂, MDEA, MDEAH⁺, HCO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, OH⁻, H⁺, equilibrium constants from fitted standard-state properties and the Rumpf–Maurer CO₂ Henry constant. Enter the solvent as N-methyldiethanolamine + water + CO₂. Liquid streams show the loading, protonated-MDEA fraction, ionic strength, equilibrium CO₂ pressure and pH; the liquid viscosity comes from a Weiland-form correlation fitted to measured MDEA viscosities with and without CO₂ (piperazine counted as MDEA by mass). Add piperazine to the components for activated MDEA (aMDEA): piperazine and its four reaction products are added to the same model, with parameters fitted here to measured CO₂ pressures over MDEA/piperazine blends and aqueous piperazine (Chen 2011, Xu 2011; 40–160 °C) — no complete published parameter set exists — and the validation page shows every point. MDEA's slow reaction is represented by a low CO₂ tray efficiency; with piperazine it can be set higher.

Sour water and aqueous electrolytes

Sour water covers NH₃, H₂S and CO₂ dissolved in water — sour water strippers, refinery and gasifier condensates, ammonia-plant process condensate. Every flash solves the ionisation of the three gases (NH₄⁺, HS⁻, S²⁻, HCO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻), ammonium carbamate and, if present, NaOH, which frees fixed ammonia. Equilibrium and Henry constants are those of Edwards et al. and Kawazuishi & Prausnitz (H₂S ionisation from the PHREEQC database); the Pitzer ion terms were fitted here to 99 measured partial pressures from 20 to 260 °C (about 12 % mean deviation in NH₃, 16 % in CO₂, 24 % in H₂S). Liquid streams show the pH, the equilibrium NH₃, H₂S and CO₂ pressures, the free-ammonia fraction and the true species.

Aqueous electrolytes covers brines, seawater, cooling water, caustic and acid solutions: the Pitzer model with the USGS PHREEQC pitzer.dat database — Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, H⁺, Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻, HSO₄⁻, OH⁻, HCO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, CO₂(aq), MgOH⁺ and MgCO₃. Give the salts as components by formula (NaCl, KCl, CaCl₂, MgCl₂, Na₂SO₄, K₂SO₄, MgSO₄, CaSO₄, NaOH, KOH, Na₂CO₃, NaHCO₃, K₂CO₃, KHCO₃, H₂SO₄) together with water and, where needed, CO₂ and HCl; light gases dissolve by Henry’s law. It reproduces PHREEQC’s water activity, osmotic coefficient and saturation indices to 4–5 digits and its pH to 0.003. Liquid streams show pH (MacInnes scale), ionic strength, water activity and the saturation indices of calcite, aragonite, dolomite, gypsum, anhydrite, halite, brucite and other minerals — a positive index means scale or crystals can form; solids are not precipitated. Solution density, viscosity and heat capacity follow Laliberté (2009). When CO₂ can leave the liquid (boiling, stripping, flashing), give carbonate alkalinity as Na₂CO₃ or K₂CO₃ plus CO₂ rather than as a bicarbonate salt: only the CO₂ component can move to the vapour. The sour water stripper and seawater evaporator examples use these two methods.

Rigorous column, Gibbs reactor, three-phase separator

The rigorous column solves the full stage-by-stage MESH equations (Naphtali–Sandholm Newton): choose stages, condenser (total, partial or none), reboiler (kettle or none), feed stages, top pressure and two specifications (reflux ratio, distillate or bottoms rate, duties, condenser temperature, a product purity or a recovery). Without condenser and reboiler it is an absorber or stripper. Trays can carry a Murphree vapour efficiency — one value for every tray, or per component (for example a low CO₂ efficiency in a chemical-solvent absorber while water stays at equilibrium); the condenser and reboiler are always equilibrium stages. Its panel shows temperature, composition and flow profiles. The Gibbs reactor finds chemical equilibrium by minimising Gibbs energy — list the species that may form, no reactions needed. Vapour fugacity coefficients come from the property method (switch to ideal gas if you need to compare), a temperature approach shifts the equilibrium to T + ΔT, graphite can be allowed to deposit at unit activity, and the equilibrium can be restricted to listed reactions each with its own approach (methane and shift approaches of a reformer). The carbon activity of the outlet gas is always reported. The three-phase separator splits vapour, light liquid and heavy (aqueous) liquid. The pipe segment marches a line in segments with a flash at every node (Darcy–Churchill friction, elevation, acceleration, fittings, homogeneous two-phase flow) and checks the erosional velocity and Mach number. With a property method, the reformers, shift converters and methanator solve their equilibria on it too. A valve’s results carry a Size this valve link to the control-valve tool, and a heat exchanger’s a Rate in HeatXpert link — both open with the stream data filled in.

Kinetic reactors (plug flow and CSTR)

The plug-flow and CSTR reactors take power-law or LHHW / Hougen–Watson kinetics: for each reaction give the stoichiometry, k0 and the activation energy (optionally about a reference temperature), optional orders (blank = elementary, negative orders allowed), a concentration or partial-pressure basis, and whether it is reversible — with a thermodynamically consistent driving force from the Gibbs-energy equilibrium constant of the databank or your own ln K correlation, or your own reverse Arrhenius rate. For catalytic kinetics add an adsorption term (Σ Kₜ Π aᵢ^eₜᵢ)^m — the Steam reformer example carries the full Xu–Froment model. Rates can be per m³ of reactor or per kg of catalyst (with the bed density). The plug-flow reactor is isothermal, adiabatic, fixed-duty or heated / cooled through the wall (U and coolant or furnace temperature) and shows axial temperature, pressure and composition profiles; fast, equilibrium-limited kinetics are integrated with a stiff (Rosenbrock) solver. Each reactor reports conversions, the heat of every reaction in the reacting phase and the residence time.

Stream properties

Stream properties on the toolbar (or the start page, the Methods panel, or Stream properties under any stream’s Stream analysis) gives the properties of one mixture at one state, laid out like a simulator’s stream table: phase fractions, molar, mass, actual and standard gas flows, molar mass, density, molar volume, Z, enthalpy, entropy, Cp, Cv, Cp/Cv, speed of sound, Joule–Thomson coefficient, viscosity, thermal conductivity and surface tension — for the mixture and for each phase — with the compositions (mole or mass), K-values and the bubble and dew points at the stream’s pressure and temperature. Type the components and amounts (or copy a flowsheet stream), the flow, temperature and pressure (or pressure and vapour fraction) and pick any property method — Peng–Robinson, NRTL, GERG-2008, steam, amine packages and the rest; it does not change the flowsheet’s own method. The mixture Cp of a two-phase stream includes the latent heat; liquid Cv, Cp/Cv and speed of sound are given by the methods with an accurate liquid equation of state (steam, reference equations, GERG-2008). Enthalpy includes the heats of formation and entropy is absolute, so compare differences with other simulators or steam tables. When the components need a method with chemistry (amine or carbonate solvents, sour water, salts, urea) and another is chosen, a warning says what is lost and offers the right method; a bubble point below 0 °C in a water-rich liquid is marked not physical. Copy for Excel or download a CSV.

Stream analysis — phase envelope, property table, binary and ternary diagrams, azeotropes

Select any stream and use Stream analysis: the phase envelope traces bubble and dew curves (with quality lines) and marks the critical point, cricondenbar and cricondentherm; the property table sweeps temperature or pressure and tabulates phase fraction, enthalpy, Cp, densities, viscosities and conductivities; the binary diagram draws T–x–y or P–x–y and y–x curves with azeotropes and the three-phase line of partially miscible pairs. The ternary map draws residue curves for any three components with the pure components and azeotropes classified as stable nodes, unstable nodes or saddles, the distillation boundaries that split the triangle into regions, and (activity methods) the liquid–liquid envelope with tie lines at a chosen temperature — the starting point for azeotropic and extractive distillation sequences. The azeotrope search lists every binary, ternary and quaternary azeotrope among up to eight components, homogeneous or heterogeneous. All use the flowsheet property method and can be copied into Excel; the flowsheet panel offers the same diagrams for any components without a stream. Checked against tabulated azeotropes in validation section 39.

Drawing

The ▾ beside T·P·F tags chooses which stream values are printed (temperature, pressure, vapour fraction, flow, molar flow, actual volume flow), whether unit duties and powers are shown, and a compact style with a legend. Below about 55 % zoom only the names are drawn, at a readable size — zoom in for the values, or hover over a stream or unit. Find (Ctrl+F) jumps to a unit or stream, Map shows an overview of the whole flowsheet, and the small tabs on the inner edges of the palette and the properties panel hide them for more room. Rotate or flip a unit with R, H, V or the buttons in its panel; drop a unit from the palette onto a stream to insert it there; select a stream and drag the square on its middle run to move it.

Sensitivity study

In the flowsheet panel, a sensitivity study steps any feed or unit input over a range and tabulates up to sixteen stream or unit results (column duties, purities, flows, temperatures …). Results appear in the Sensitivity tab as a chart and a table you can copy into Excel.

Calculators

A calculator (flowsheet panel) reads stream or unit results, evaluates your own formulas — one “name = expression” per line, with + − * / ^, comparisons, if(), min, max, sqrt, exp, ln … — and writes the answers into feeds or unit specifications: a steam flow set from the methane flow, a cooling-water flow from a duty, a split from an analyser. It runs after the units it reads and before the units it sets; one that reads a result its own outputs change is iterated with the flowsheet. Values are in K, bar(a), kg/h and kW.

Design specs

Press Specs to make the simulator vary a feed or unit input until a result hits a target — for example process air until H₂/N₂ = 3, or a flash temperature for a given water content. Specs are solved on the server together with the whole flowsheet.

Optimisation

In the flowsheet panel, Optimisation varies 1–6 feed or unit inputs (each between bounds) to minimise or maximise an objective — a weighted sum of stream or unit results, or the total annual cost when costing is on — subject to up to 8 limits such as a purity ≥ 99.5 % or an outlet temperature ≤ 120 °C. One variable is searched by Brent's method, several by a bounded Nelder–Mead simplex; every point is a whole-flowsheet solve with the design specs met, and a point that breaks a limit is never preferred to one that meets it. The results shown after the run are at the optimum; Copy optimum to inputs writes it into the feeds and units. The Convergence tab plots and tabulates every evaluation. A variable cannot also be varied by an active design spec or set by a calculator. The search finds a local optimum — start it from a sensible point, and widen the bounds if a variable ends on one.

Costing

Switch on Costing in the flowsheet panel to size and cost the units after each run by Turton's module-costing correlations, escalated with the CE plant cost index (2023 average 797.9 by default) and converted to your currency: exchangers from their duty, LMTD and a typical U (or yours), flash drums by Souders–Brown with five minutes' liquid hold-up, columns at 80 % of flooding with sieve trays, pumps and compressors with their electric drives from the shaft power, fired heaters from the absorbed duty, reactors from their volume (enter it under Costing in the unit's panel for equilibrium reactors). Heaters are put on the lowest steam level that is hot enough (else a fired heater), coolers on cooling water, chilled water or refrigeration, and the utilities are priced over the operating hours. The Costs tab lists every item with its size, material, pressure and bare-module factors, the utilities, and the purchased, bare-module, total-module and grassroots capital, the capital annualised over the plant life, and the total annual cost. A unit's panel sets its equipment type, material of construction and sizing basis. Study-estimate accuracy; the prices are examples.

Pinch analysis

The Pinch tab takes every heating and cooling duty of the last run — heaters and coolers, condensers and reboilers, intercoolers, reactor and flash duties (utility-served) and both sides of process exchangers — and computes the minimum hot and cold utility for a chosen ΔTmin by the problem-table algorithm, the pinch temperatures, the composite and grand composite curves, and the utility heaters below or coolers above the pinch that cause the excess. With a property method, heater and exchanger curves are flashed along their path, so boiling and condensation show as plateaus. Untick a duty that cannot take part in heat recovery.

Cases

The Cases tab saves the inputs and main results of a run as a named case (up to 10). Change the inputs, run and save again, then compare the cases side by side — a summary (duties, design-spec values, costs, optimum, products), unit duties, any one stream, or the inputs that differ — with the difference from a base case under each value. Restore loads a case's inputs back (undo returns). Cases are saved with the flowsheet file and in this browser, not in share links; the report can include the comparison.

Recycle loops

Loops are detected automatically. The fewest streams that break every loop are torn and iterated — two Wegstein steps, then Broyden's quasi-Newton method — until they converge (relative tolerance 1e-5 by default). Torn streams are drawn dashed. You can give a stream an initial estimate from its properties panel; when the estimated streams break every loop they are the ones torn. A flowsheet too large for one server call is continued automatically over further calls. The Convergence tab plots each loop's error pass by pass (marked by how each pass was started — guess, substitution, Wegstein or Broyden), lists the last change in flow, temperature, pressure and the worst component of every torn stream, and shows the design-spec Newton steps (scaled residual, step length, Jacobian) and feedback-calculator passes. Choose Broyden or Wegstein only under Run options in the flowsheet panel.

Results

The stream table shows T, P, flows, enthalpy and composition (mass or mole basis, flows or fractions) for every stream; copy it straight into Excel or download CSV; on large flowsheets filter it by name or unit, or show only feeds and products or the streams of the selected units. The Summary tab (opened after every run) gives the run status and warnings, overall mass closure, the energy-balance check, heating / cooling and power totals, the largest duties and the product streams (and, when they are on, the optimum and the total annual cost). Turn on T·P·F tags to print results on the drawing, and use Report for a printable PDF. Report opens the report builder: pick the sections, the streams, the units and the stream properties, then download a Word file (.docx) or open a print view to save as PDF — both with multicalci.com, the date and the page number on every page. The Issues tab ends with a specification check of every unit: the specification chosen, the values given, what is missing and any value entered that the chosen specification does not use.

Editing several units

Shift- or Ctrl-click units to select several, Shift-drag on empty canvas to box-select, or Ctrl+A for all. Drag any selected unit to move them together; the panel aligns them (left, centre, right, top, middle, bottom) and spaces them evenly. Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V copy and paste units with the streams between them — also into a flowsheet in another tab — and Ctrl+D duplicates; inlets and outlets that were connected outside the copied group get new feed and product streams. Copy a stream and paste it onto a feed stream to give the feed the same conditions and composition (a calculated stream is pasted on a mass basis).

Keyboard

F5 run · F1 assumptions and valid ranges of the selected unit (or of the property method) · Del delete selection · Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo / redo · Ctrl+C / X / V / D copy, cut, paste, duplicate · Ctrl+A select all · arrow keys nudge the selection · Ctrl+F find a unit or stream · Esc cancel / stop a run / deselect · Ctrl+wheel or pinch to zoom, drag empty canvas to pan.

Validation

The property methods, the three-phase flash, the rigorous column and the phase envelope are checked against DWSIM and the open-source thermo library, the Gibbs reactor against NIST-JANAF, the kinetic reactors against exact solutions, the CO₂ packages against measured MEA vapour pressures, IDAES and USGS PHREEQC, the tray efficiencies against the exact Murphree–Kremser solution, and the energy balance and recycle / design-spec solver against independent references — see how the simulator is validated.

Scope

Results are for preliminary engineering. Property methods are only as good as their parameters (Peng–Robinson without fitted kij over-predicts water in hydrocarbon liquids; ChemSep activity parameters come from vapour–liquid data). The MEA model over-predicts the CO₂ pressure at high loadings (above about 0.35 mol/mol at regenerator temperatures, 0.5 at absorber temperatures), and the hot-carbonate Pitzer parameters are extrapolated above about 100 °C and are held at their stability limit in very strong, highly converted solutions (near KHCO₃ crystallisation); tray efficiencies stand in for mass-transfer rates. The McCabe–Thiele and Kremser columns are shortcut models and the classic urea units are tuned empirical correlations; the urea synthesis method omits biuret and urea hydrolysis, and its liquid density and viscosity are estimates. Pseudo-component properties carry the accuracy of their correlations (critical pressure about 6 %). Verify against plant data before using results for design.
