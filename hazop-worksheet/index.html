How to use the HAZOP worksheet builder

  1. Enter the study details (optional): study title, P&ID or MOC reference, date and team.
  2. Add a node. Give it a name and a design intent, for example "Transfer 40 m³/h naphtha at 8 barg from T-101 to column C-201".
  3. Choose the equipment type: pipeline, pump, compressor, storage tank, pressure vessel, heat exchanger or column.
  4. Choose the fluid hazards and phase. These set the consequence of a release and its severity.
  5. Tick the safeguards installed today: instruments, alarms, trips, relief valves, check valves, procedures. Ticked items are credited; an expected safeguard that is missing raises a recommendation.
  6. Review the draft with your team. Edit any text, change severity or likelihood, swap a cause for another one from the library, add your own causes and mark each row as reviewed.
  7. Track the recommendations in the Recommendations tab: give each an owner, a target date and a status.
  8. Export to Excel or print. Use Save file to keep an editable copy of the study.

HAZOP terms used in the worksheet

Node
A section of the plant studied as one unit, usually one equipment item with its connected lines, between clear boundaries on the P&ID.
Design intent
What the node is meant to do at normal conditions: flow, pressure, temperature, level, composition. Every deviation is measured against it.
Guide word
A word such as No, More, Less or Reverse that prompts the team to imagine a departure from the design intent.
Parameter
A process variable such as flow, pressure, temperature or level.
Deviation
Guide word + parameter, for example "No flow" or "High pressure". The worksheet is grouped by deviation.
Cause
Why the deviation could happen: equipment failure, control failure, human error or an external event. Causes are listed most likely first, with their typical frequency per year.
Consequence
What could happen if the cause occurs and no protection works, written for the fluid hazards of the node.
Safeguard
Protection already in the design: an alarm, trip, relief valve, check valve, dike or procedure. Only safeguards you ticked are listed.
Severity (S)
How bad the worst credible consequence is, from 1 (minor) to 5 (catastrophic).
Likelihood (L)
How often the cause is expected, from 1 (remote) to 5 (frequent), set from its typical frequency.
Risk
Severity combined with likelihood on the risk matrix: Low, Medium, High or Very high. Shown before safeguards and with the listed safeguards.
Recommendation
An action to reduce the risk further, with an owner, target date and status.

Guide words and deviations

The guide words follow IEC 61882, the international standard for HAZOP studies.

Guide wordMeaningExample deviations
No / NoneComplete absence of the design intentNo flow
More / HighQuantitative increaseMore flow, high pressure, high temperature, high level
Less / LowQuantitative decreaseLess flow, low pressure, low level
ReverseOpposite directionReverse flow

How risk is ranked

Each row has a severity and a likelihood from 1 to 5. The risk matrix turns the pair into Low, Medium, High or Very high. The default matrix ranks severity × likelihood: up to 3 is Low, 4 to 8 Medium, 9 to 14 High and 15 or more Very high. You can change any cell in the Risk matrix tab to match your company procedure.

Risk is shown twice. Before safeguards uses the likelihood of the cause alone. With safeguards keeps the same severity and lowers the likelihood for the safeguards you ticked: one level for an alarm, auto-start, trip, NRV or similar device, and two levels for a PSV or vent, up to three levels in total. This is a screening estimate; confirm critical scenarios with a LOPA study.

Frequently asked questions

Is the HAZOP worksheet builder free?

Yes. It is free to use with no sign-up. You can build as many nodes as you need and export the worksheet to Excel.

Is my HAZOP study uploaded anywhere?

No. The study is saved in your own browser. To draft the rows, only the equipment type, fluid hazard, phase and ticked features are sent to the library. Node names, design intent and your edits are never sent. Use Save file to keep a copy or move the study to another computer.

Can I use the draft as my final HAZOP?

The draft is a starting point for a HAZOP team. The team must go through every row, add causes specific to the plant, confirm severity and likelihood against the company risk matrix, and agree the recommendations. It does not replace a facilitated HAZOP study.

Can I change the risk matrix?

Yes. Open the Risk matrix tab, choose Edit risk levels and click any cell to change its level so the matrix matches your company procedure. All risk rankings in the study update at once.

Which equipment types are covered?

Pipelines, pumps, compressors, storage tanks, pressure vessels, heat exchangers and columns, for any fluid. You choose the fluid hazards (flammable, toxic, corrosive, hot, cryogenic, environmental or non-hazardous) and the phase.

How do I share the study with my team?

Export Excel gives a worksheet, a recommendations list and the node basis that anyone can open. Save file gives a .hazop.json file that a colleague can load with Open to continue editing.

This tool is in beta. The draft is generated from a generic library of causes, typical frequencies and safeguard credits; it must be reviewed by a competent HAZOP team before use. Replace generic frequencies with site data where available. Send feedback or report a problem.
